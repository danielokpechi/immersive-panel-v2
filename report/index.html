The evidence base

What 14 fans told us

Every change traces back to Fan Research: What We Learned — 14 moderated sessions with fans, multi-sport fans and non-fans. The bottom line was uncomfortable: the idea had a real audience, but its strongest moment wasn't the one it was built for, and the retention engine was invisible to everyone.

Blocker

XP & rewards are invisible

Not one participant understood XP unprompted — the retention engine produced zero motivation. 14/14

Blocker

The cold screen doesn't land

With no context it reads as a live-score app, a blog, or a betting feature. 11/14

Blocker

Team identity is illegible

No flags, colours or names — it assumes you know both sides. 3/3 non-fans

Annoyance

First-screen overload

One fan counted 11 nav options; whole features get missed. 9/14

Annoyance

"Prediction" reads as betting

A trust-friction point before the feature is even used. 4/14

Annoyance

Layout lacks hierarchy

Fan photos surfaced before scores; the changing layout never explains itself. 5–8/14

Part 1 · The operator studio

Panel Studio — from a generic SaaS console to a club matchday desk

The studio is where the club authors and drives a panel. v1 was a capable but generic dark-purple dashboard. v2 is a Manchester City workspace: real competitions, real templates, and a control room that drives the actual fan device live.

1 · Dashboard

v1 lists nine invented demo panels on a purple gradient with a stock "BoltOS" identity. v2 is a club workspace — City crest, an admin identity, and six real competition panels (league, cups, women's, tour) with live/draft status, venue tags and square-cornered City cards.

Before · v1
After · v2

2 · Create-panel wizard

Both walk five steps, but the spine changed. v1 asks Experience → Sport → Team → Modules → Brand, where modules are global on/off toggles. v2 asks Experience → Competition → Template → Blocks & states → Brand — start from a real City template, then compose each match state separately. That serves the finding that "the changing layout is the core bet, and it needs explaining": the layout per state is now an authored decision.

3 · Panel builder

v1's editor is a name/brand form plus a flat grid of global module switches. v2's builder keeps the live preview but adds per-session module stacks — Warm-up, Build-up, First half, Half-time, Second half, Full-time each get their own ordered blocks — plus image-pack and palette control.

4 · Stats

Same metrics, re-grounded. v1 sits on the purple theme with emoji labels; v2 reads as a club analytics view — City navy, "last event vs previous home fixture", per-module engagement ranked.

5 · Control room

The biggest functional leap. v1's console previews a mock phone. v2's control room drives the real fan client over a realtime channel: a true "live monitor" mirrors what fans see, sessions jump every device at once, and triggers (Goal, VAR, sub, card, MOTM, push poll, reaction, flash sale) "fire instantly to 5,298 devices".

Part 2 · The fan app

Matchday Companion — rebuilt around what fans actually reached for

The fan side is where the research bit hardest. v1 was a dark, generic "Immersive Panel". v2 is a Man City matchday companion — navy, square-cornered, crest-led — that leads with the two things fans went to unprompted (the score and the chat) and finally makes the reward layer visible.

The cold open — the first thing a fan sees

Research · Blocker (11/14): "The cold read fails… reads as a live-score app, a blog, or a betting feature." "It looks like a blog for football matches." And 3/3 non-fans: team identity illegible.

v1 opens on a generic header, a "Start Matchday" button and a Reads/Shop carousel — and a Quests block that literally reads "Quests unlock on matchday · Start Matchday to begin earning XP". v2 opens on a City-vs-Madrid score bug with both crests and recent form, the first-leg recap with a Watch highlights button, and a one-line frame: "Tonight's companion: follow the game, talk to other City fans, and earn towards a seat upgrade while you watch. Nothing to install."

The home screen — from 11 options to one curated scroll

Research · Annoyance (9/14): "First-screen overload — one fan counted 11 nav options; features get missed." Move #3: lead with the score and the chat; push everything else into a hub.

v1 stacks modules and a bottom navbar all competing at once. v2 is a single ranked scroll: score → fan-chat preview ("308 talking now") → Match Intel → an "Also tonight" hub where every remaining module is one clear tap with a plain-English line and a badge.

Making the reward visible — the single biggest fix

Research · Blocker (14/14): "XP & rewards invisible — kills the entire quest layer." "I don't know why I have 325 XP or why I should be bothered." Move #1: show what XP earns you, on every quest card, with a progress bar.

In v1, XP only appears if you dig into the profile. In v2 the reward is everywhere the quest is: each quest shows its exact XP (prediction · 120 XP, poll · 40 XP, photo · 25 XP), the live home carries a Matchday Rewards · Tier 2 strip, and the profile turns XP into a concrete goal — "1,240 / 2,000 XP · 760 to a seat upgrade", plus a claimed "£5 food credit at 500 XP".

Fan chat — the universal pull, kept clean

Research (12/14): "Fan chat is the universal pull — the only feature fans and non-fans alike went to unprompted." Concern (John): predictions bleed into the chat feed and clutter it.

v2 makes chat the anchor: it previews on the home, opens to a single unified terrace thread with quick-reply chips, and keeps the score bug pinned above it so the can't-watch-live viewer never loses the game. Predictions and polls are their own hub cards now, resolving the clutter point.

IRIS — from mid-match chatbot to prepared match intel

Research (9/14): "IRIS is misread and misplaced. A chatbot during a loud live match reads as wrong. Three people independently reframed it as an insight engine, best post-match." Move #5.

v1's IRIS is a full-screen "What do you want to know?" chatbot — the mid-match blank box fans rejected. v2 leads with Match Intel: "The briefing, already written" — win probability, form & xG, and pre-written "watch for" cards. IRIS is still there, but as a grounded briefing you can ask into, not an empty prompt.

Highlights, predictions & the buried favourites

Research: highlights were the most-wanted missing feature (8/14); "prediction" read as betting (4/14); the floor plan was a buried favourite, and food "belongs near the floor plan".

v1 had no video and surfaced the floor plan last. v2 adds Watch highlights and a Reads desk; reframes predictions as "Call the score and first scorer · 120 XP" (a game with a reward, not a bet); and promotes the floor plan to "Your seat" — a stand map with your block, walk-in route, nearest kiosk and exit — with Order food "to seat" right beside it.

The score as an event, and a profile you can leave

Research (UI): "no micro-interactions — the score updating should feel like an event, not a quiet number change." Plus: a stuck profile page with no back button.

A goal in v2 fires a full-width "GOAL — Foden 1–0" banner, a flash-drop offer and a live timeline — the score is now an event, reinforced by new micro-animations. And every sub-screen, the profile included, carries a persistent Home back button plus theme and alert controls.

The through-line

v1 proved the concept had an audience. v2 answers the research point by point: it makes the reward visible, frames the cold open, leads with the score and the chat, adds the highlights fans asked for, and turns IRIS into the post-match intel three people reframed it as — all in the club's own identity, driven live from the control room.
